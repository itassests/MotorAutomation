Section 1

Executive Summary

Every motor insurer pays intermediaries commission from a periodically-revised grid that varies by insurer, product, cover, vehicle attributes, geography, and business type. These grids arrive as inconsistent spreadsheets and PDFs. RateExtract turns them into a single, auditable engine that assigns the correct commission rate to each policy, computes the payout, reconciles it against the operator's own records, and surfaces exceptions.

The platform is operated on a monthly payout cycle. For a cycle, tens of thousands of policies are rated in bulk against the effective grids, then reconciled against the Premium Register and commission statements. The core challenge — and the bulk of this document — is that no two insurers structure their grid the same way. Section 6 records the per-insurer logic and the decisions taken to make each one match what the insurer actually pays.

GOAL
Assign a defensible commission rate to every motor policy in a cycle, matching the insurer's published grid and the operator's paid records, with a full audit trail from source grid → matched rule → rate → payout.
Section 2

Purpose & Scope

In scope

  • Ingestion and version-management of insurer commission grids (rate cards) — Excel, HTML-as-xls, and PDF-derived tables.
  • The rate-matching engine: resolving each policy to the correct rule across insurer / product / cover / vehicle band / region / business type.
  • Bulk calculation of commission for a monthly cycle, with per-policy overrides and payout finalisation.
  • Reconciliation against the Premium Register (PR) and insurer commission statements.
  • Grid-health tooling so monthly grid refreshes are self-service (audit, upload validation, generic flat-grid loader).
  • Downstream exports: the Luca commission-rule file, and the Policy Push to the agent mobile app.
  • Operational tooling: single-policy lookup, OCR bulk tracker generation.

Out of scope

  • Policy issuance, premium calculation, and the core policy-admin system (these are upstream, in Prarambh).
  • The mobile app itself and the OneInsure OCR/token APIs (consumed as external services).
  • Non-motor lines of business (health, life) — the engine is motor-only (InsuranceType = 16).

4.1 Rate-card ingestion & management

An operator uploads a grid; it is parsed to rate_rules under a rate_cards row with an effective window. Uploads are content-sniffed — a portal "xls" that is really an HTML <table> is parsed as HTML, not read as garbled text. Re-uploading a corrected grid creates a newer card; the matching engine's newest-per-cover-class selection (§5.2) means it supersedes the prior card for the covers it contains without disturbing others. Where a grid cannot be represented as flat single-rate rules, it is handled by an insurer resolver or the generic flat-grid loader (§4.5, §7).

FR-1
A corrected grid re-uploaded mid-cycle takes effect on re-computation for its own cover-classes and effective window only; already-correct policies on other covers are unchanged.

4.2 Matching engine

The heart of the platform — resolving each policy to the right rule. Its cross-insurer principles are in §5 and every insurer-specific behaviour in §6.

4.3 Bulk calculation & the payout cycle

For a cycle, the engine reads the period's policies, rates each one, and writes a full computed row to cycle_bulk_rows. The commission maths runs on a premium base chosen by product and cover:

  • GCV / PCV / MISC → the net premium base;
  • Private car / two-wheeler → OD + add-on for Comp/SAOD, or TP for TP-only;
  • Two-leg insurers (New India, National) instead apply OD% to the OD premium and TP% to the TP premium and sum the legs.

From the matched rate the engine derives three figures the payout is built on:

FigureMeaning
IncomeTotal commission earned on the policy = rate% × premium base (or the two-leg sum).
OutgoingWhat is paid out to the agent = outgoing% × base, where outgoing% = rate% − effective margin%.
SavingsWhat the company retains = Income − Outgoing (the margin the platform applies on top of the base rate).

Margins come from margin rules (with special-rate overrides and global uplifts layered on). Any of rate / margin / outgoing can be overridden per policy by an inline edit; overrides are stored separately and survive recomputation, so re-running a cycle never discards manual corrections. Rows can be excluded from payout or moved to another cycle. A finalised cycle locks — rates and actions freeze — and settlement is tracked against a UTR/bank upload (paid amount, UTR, paid date). Each row also carries compliance context (e.g. POS-agent + IDV threshold) and the agent's POS-match status.

FR-3
Recompute is idempotent per insurer and preserves overrides, notes, paid status and exclusions; finalisation is irreversible without an explicit re-open.

4.4 Reconciliation — Premium Register & statements

Rated output is checked against two operator records:

  • Premium Register (PR). The operator's own premium export (pr_rows / pr_uploads) is matched by policy number (with insurer-specific key variants). It surfaces PR Net / Gross / OD / TP alongside the engine's figures, and is also a source of fields the staging feed lacks — most notably Royal's OD discount (FINALDISCOUNT) and fallback tonnage / region.
  • Commission statements. The insurer's own commission statement (statement_rows / statement_uploads) is reconciled against the computed income; each policy lands in a status bucket (matched / variance / not-in-statement) with the variance amount, feeding a recon summary.
FR-4
Every policy carries a reconciliation status and, where applicable, a signed variance against the operator's paid/stated amount, so exceptions are a filtered list rather than a manual hunt.

4.5 Grid robustness & audit

Because grids are refreshed monthly by non-developers, three layers keep the refresh self-service:

  1. Per-cycle grid audit — flags no-rule, stale-card and zero-rate clusters per insurer × product, so a mis-ingested month is visible before payout.
  2. Upload validation — at upload, flags 0-rule sheets, scratch/checks tabs, and scrambled columns with a clear banner, so junk (e.g. a "cv checks" scratch tab slurping hundreds of thousands of bogus rules) never lands silently.
  3. Generic flat-grid loader — a config-driven parser (config + extracted data per insurer) that reads and resolves flat tables without bespoke code and re-extracts on upload; the strategic direction is to migrate resolvers onto it.

4.6 Policy lookup

A single-policy trace by tracker or policy number: it shows the extracted parameters, the resolved region/cluster, the matched rule and rate, the effective card chosen, the PR discount, and the policy's issue and start dates. It is the dispute-resolution tool — it answers "why did this policy get this rate?" reproducibly.

4.8 OCR bulk upload

An operator uploads ZIP folders of policy PDFs. For each PDF the system, in a durable background worker: generates a tracker number (via the tracker SP with the operator's derived identity), OCRs the PDF through the OneInsure two-step API to extract the policy number, writes the (tracker, policy) result, and calls the finalisation SP that submits the tracker to the underwriting team. Progress is per-folder; results download per folder or as one consolidated date-range export (Policy No + Tracker No across every folder in a window), since folder-by-folder matching is impractical. State lives in the DB so processing resumes after any restart.

4.9 Policy push to the mobile app

Motor policies (submission date ≥ 1-Apr-2026) are pushed to the agent mobile app via the OneInsure API — a token call then a multipart data call per policy. A one-time backfill seeds the whole history; thereafter a guarded daily job enqueues the previous day's submissions. A throttled background worker drains the queue: it builds the ~70-field payload from the live motor view (dates DD-MM-YYYY, booleans 0/1, business type motor_*), fetches a cached token, and pushes. The API upserts by policy number, so retries and corrections are safe; a policy without a number yet is deferred until issued. An admin dashboard shows daily/monthly/yearly submitted / pushed / failed / awaiting counts, with per-row error, repush, and correct-and-repush. It ships gated off, dry-run by default, and only runs on one host.

FR-9
The push is resumable and idempotent (unique key + upsert-by-policy-number); PII leaves the trust boundary only under an explicit, authorised, human-initiated go-live.
Section 5

Rate-Matching Logic — General Principles

These cross-insurer rules govern the engine before any insurer-specific handling in §6.

5.1 Cover class from premiums

The cover is derived from the OD and TP premium legs, not a text field: SATP when OD≤0 (TP-only), SAOD when OD>0 and TP≤0, Comprehensive/Package when both are positive. This drives which column/segment of a grid applies.

5.2 Effective-date card selection

A policy takes the grid effective on its risk-start date, universally across insurers (not just the two it began with). The lookup selects the newest card per cover-class (Comp / SAOD / TP resolved from rate type + segment), so a June-14 card supersedes a June-1 card for June-14+ policies while May policies still take the earlier card. A soft fallback re-queries without the date filter if no dated rule matches, so a mis-dated card never forces a no-rule.

Decision. When multiple cards are effective, "newest per cover-class" — not "newest overall" — is the selector, because insurers revise one cover at a time. Cards should carry an effective_to only when a true successor exists; otherwise the newest-per-cover-class logic hands off cleanly on its own.

5.3 Vehicle-type mapping

A policy's vehicle type is mapped to a product family (CAR / TW / GCV / PCV / MISC). GCV/PCV are tested before CAR — a segment like GCV - 4W 12-20Tn contains the token "4W" and would otherwise mis-map to CAR. A guard reclassifies a 2-seat, ≤350cc "car" as a two-wheeler (mis-typed motorcycles). MISC construction equipment (cranes, loaders) labelled "Tractor" is kept out of the Tractor bucket; goods-carrying MISC with a recoverable GVW is reclassified to GCV.

5.4 Band boundaries

For both tonnage and vehicle-age bands, the lower band owns a shared edge (e.g. exactly 40T belongs to "20–40", not "40–45"). A precise GVW only refines a coarse category band when it falls strictly inside it, so the operator's classification governs at the edges.

5.5 Business type ≠ new vehicle

"New business" (new to the insurer) is distinct from a "new vehicle" (age-based). A New-business policy on an existing vehicle is a Rollover, not Brand New. New-vehicle status is derived from vehicle age (manufactured date preferred over registration), falling back to NCB as a proxy only when age is unknown.

5.6 Fuel, region & dedup

  • Fuel. Factory gas kits arriving as INBUILT map to the CNG family; "Other than Diesel" grid columns score against any non-diesel fuel.
  • Region. Resolved from the vehicle RTO via rto_mappings to an insurer-specific region/cluster, with per-insurer overrides (some insurers key on booking location or district — §6).
  • Selection. Among surviving rules, a scored match with a stated tie-break picks the primary rate; declines (grid-listed excluded RTOs/segments) force a null rate rather than a fallback.

Section 7

Data Sources & Model

Three databases back the platform, kept deliberately separate.

StoreHoldsRole
RateExtractrate_cards, rate_rules, rto_mappings, cycle_bulk_rows, margins, PR/statement uploads, and the Node-owned job queues.The engine's own store — rates, matched output, and orchestration state.
Prarambh (UAT)tmp_PrarambhData — the staging policy feed the bulk engine reads.Source of policy attributes and premiums for rating.
Prarambh (Live)TRN_PrarambhMain + the motor download view; the authoritative issued-policy record.Read-only source for lookup, OCR, and the mobile-app push payload.

Rate cards are versioned by effective date; rules carry region, segment, make, fuel, cc/weight/age/seating bands, rate type and value. Where a grid can't be represented as flat single-rate rules, an insurer resolver (code + JSON) computes the rate instead — the durable direction is the generic flat-grid loader (config + extracted data), which re-extracts on upload and retires bespoke resolvers.

Section 8

Non-Functional Requirements

NFR — Auditability
Every rated policy traces source grid → matched rule → resolved region/band → rate → payout, reproducible on demand via the lookup screen.
NFR — Performance & scale
Cycles rate tens of thousands of policies; queries use lean projections (never SELECT * over the wide policy tables), pagination, and batched writes. Background workers (OCR, policy push) are throttled and must not degrade interactive use.
NFR — Durability & resumability
Long-running work (OCR, policy push, backfill) is queue-backed in the DB with per-item status, so a restart or wake resumes without duplication (unique keys) or loss.
NFR — Correctness under change
Config/resolver refactors that must not alter output are proven byte-identical against the prior version before shipping; grid re-ingests are diffed against the baseline (no-rule and match-rate deltas).
NFR — Security
Admin-gated operations (payout, policy push) behind authenticated roles; secrets in environment config, never in source or git; PII sent to external services only under explicit, authorised, human-run action.
Section 9

Assumptions, Dependencies & Open Items

Assumptions

  • Grids are refreshed monthly and re-uploaded per cycle; the operator provides the source files.
  • The operator's Premium Register and statements are the ground truth for reconciliation; the insurer circular is ground truth for rates.
  • Motor scope only (InsuranceType = 16); dummy trackers (/DUM/) are excluded from downstream flows.

Dependencies

  • Upstream Prarambh policy data quality (tonnage, fuel, dates are frequently sparse and are recovered via fallbacks).
  • External OneInsure services: OCR (tracker/policy extraction) and the mobile-app token + data-push APIs.

Open items

  • Migration of remaining bespoke resolvers to the generic flat-grid loader.
  • True Excel-upload self-service for the two-leg insurers (New India, National) — currently JSON-editable + restart.
  • Full self-service for PDF-sourced grids (United India GCV/PCV/TW state grids).
  • Policy-push payload fields not present in the motor view (add-on flags, document bytes) — sent blank pending confirmation of which are required.
